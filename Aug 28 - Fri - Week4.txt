#######POST 1 morning

The number that may explain the exception is 90 basis points.

$MRVL beat revenue. It beat earnings. Guidance came in above consensus.

But gross margin guidance still slipped 90 basis points as AI-related R&D and compensation costs climbed.

So $MRVL may not have broken yesterday's rule.

It may have added a second condition to it.

Yesterday, $NVDA, $CRM and $CRWD showed that clearing the growth bar can trigger a re-rating. $MRVL is testing what happens when that growth comes with deteriorating economics underneath it.

The market may be asking a more demanding question now: not just how fast can you grow, but what does that growth leave behind?

Watch whether $MRVL's 7.6% after-hours decline holds through the regular session. If it does, margins may be the next filter this market starts applying to companies that otherwise clear every headline expectation. 🍌


#######POST 2 morning

The underlying quarter was actually solid, deal chatter aside.

That's the real test now.

Watch whether $PYPL finds a floor above where it traded before the rumor started, or whether today's 16% move erases the quarter's entire gain. If it does, the business never really re-rated.

Only the story did. 🍌


#######POST 3 midday

Two stocks opened down double digits this morning for completely different reasons. Only one of them stopped falling.

$MRVL opened almost exactly where its after-hours move left it, down about **7%**. The market had one specific number to price: gross margin guidance came in **90bps** light. Once investors saw the number, they set a price and, so far, largely stayed there.

$PYPL didn't get that kind of certainty. Its decline widened from around **12%** before the bell to closer to **15%** at the open. There was no earnings miss to measure. Just a deal that never had a confirmed offer being unwound in real time.

A known bad number can be priced once.

A speculative premium built on an outcome that never existed has to keep searching for where it belongs.

Watch whether $PYPL keeps drifting into the afternoon while $MRVL holds near its **7%** loss. If it does, these aren't two similar selloffs.

One is repricing a fact.

The other is still discovering how much of its price was a story. 🍌


#######POST 4 midday

Kevin Warsh spent his first Jackson Hole keynote refusing to give the market the one thing it explicitly wanted: a reaction function.

He acknowledged inflation running above target and called it the Fed's predominant focus. Beyond that, he committed to very little.

Yields rose anyway.

The interesting part may be what wasn't in the speech.

For two weeks, **Treasury's expanding long-bond buyback efforts** have been sitting underneath the long end of the market. Today, Warsh gave investors no indication of how the Fed sees that broader intervention — no support for it, no distance from it, no acknowledgment at all.

That leaves the market to fill in the gap.

We usually grade Fed speeches by what gets said about rates. This one may be getting priced through something more subtle: the absence of confirmation that monetary and fiscal policy are moving in the same direction.

Watch the **30-year**, the **dollar**, and **gold** into the close. If yields hold higher while the dollar strengthens and gold keeps giving back recent gains, the market may be treating Warsh's silence as its own kind of answer. 🍌



#######POST 5 afternoon

A hawkish Fed speech was supposed to hit stocks. This one didn't.

Warsh told Jackson Hole that inflation is still running above target and that price stability remains the Fed's predominant focus. Traders heard enough to push September hike odds from 35% to roughly 56–60% within the hour. The dollar jumped. The 2-year yield hit its highest level since late July.

The S&P dipped, then reversed into a 0.3% gain. The Dow and Nasdaq followed.

The explanation may be sitting further down the curve.

While the 2-year was repricing a more hawkish Fed, the 30-year actually fell a few basis points. The front end moved. The long end didn't follow.

That matters more than the headline contradiction. Stocks can absorb a Fed repricing if the borrowing costs that matter for valuations over the next decade remain contained. And after two weeks of Treasury buyback intervention, the long end is still behaving like it has a different force acting on it.

We keep asking whether the Fed is hawkish or dovish. Today's curve gave a more useful answer: **the Fed can move one part of the market without moving all of it.**

Watch the 30-year into the close. If it starts following the 2-year higher, today's rally may have to revisit its assumptions. If it stays anchored while the front end keeps repricing, the split we've been tracking all week just became much harder to dismiss. 🍌


#######POST 6 afternoon

The dollar is doing exactly what a hawkish Fed speech should make it do. Gold and Bitcoin aren't.

The dollar is holding near a three-month high after Warsh pushed September hike odds from **35% to roughly 56–60%**. Yet gold is still above yesterday's level, while Bitcoin is holding near **$79,500** and continues to outperform stocks for the month.

That matters because it weakens the explanation we've been using for two weeks: dollar weakness alone can't explain today's resilience.

Something slower may be doing the work instead — the deficit, Treasury's bond-market intervention, or the market's view of fiscal credibility.

There's still time for this to change today. But if gold and Bitcoin hold these levels through the final minutes, Monday becomes the cleaner test: can the trade survive an entire weekend with a genuinely hawkish Fed message sitting in the background? 🍌



#######POST 7 afternoon

$MRVL didn't stop falling when investors found the margin problem. It kept falling when they found the timeline problem.

The stock has moved from roughly **7% down** at the open to about **11% down** this afternoon, despite beating both revenue and earnings. The **90bps** gross-margin shortfall explained the first leg. It didn't explain the second.

The missing piece: Marvell's custom AI chip partnership with Google isn't expected to contribute meaningfully until **fiscal 2029**. Investors aren't just repricing what AI costs. They're repricing when the payoff arrives.

That's the sharper lesson developing today. Clearing consensus isn't enough when the market has already priced a faster timeline.

$AVGO reports Tuesday. Its AI-silicon timeline may now matter as much as its numbers.

Watch whether $MRVL holds near this **11%** loss into the final minutes. If it does, the timeline—not the margin miss—may be what investors remember from today. 🍌



#######MARKET CLOSE/CENSUS POST

**MARKET STRUCTURE CENSUS — AUG. 28, 2026**

Warsh gave the market its clearest hawkish signal of the week. The market responded exactly where it was supposed to — the dollar jumped to a three-month high, September hike odds roughly doubled, and the 2-year yield reached its highest level in a month.

Then the rest of the market refused to follow.

The 30-year barely moved. Gold finished above Thursday's level. Bitcoin held near **$79,500**. The assets that had spent two weeks trading the debasement story survived the strongest dollar test we've seen yet.

That wasn't the only assumption that broke this week.

$NVDA initially fell despite beating revenue by **$4B** and earnings by **13 cents**, then reversed as its call pointed to strong FY28 growth. $MRVL beat revenue and earnings and guided above consensus, yet fell from roughly **7% down** to about **11%** as investors realized its Google AI-chip revenue may not arrive meaningfully until **fiscal 2029**. $PYPL lost roughly **15%** when a deal that was never formally offered disappeared.

Different companies. Different catalysts. Same deeper lesson: the market is becoming less interested in whether the headline is good or bad and more interested in **what was already priced, what changes next, and how long investors have to wait for the payoff.**

Even the macro tape followed that rule. Oil fell for a fifth straight session despite escalating Iran rhetoric because the market found physical evidence of normalizing Hormuz traffic. Treasury pushed harder on the long end, yet the 30-year barely responded. Warsh moved the front end sharply, but couldn't pull the long end with it.

This week didn't give us one market story. It gave us a market becoming harder to fool with the first headline.

Next week starts with the question Friday couldn't answer: **does that discipline survive when the catalysts disappear and investors have to price the underlying story on their own?** 🍌
