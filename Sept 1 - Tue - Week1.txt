#######POST 1 morning

Three weeks after Treasury expanded its long-end buybacks, the 30-year is back around 5.28%. The 10-year is at 4.79%, its highest since January 2025.

And September hike odds are now above 65%... up sharply from before Jackson Hole.

Treasury has answered every wobble with a bigger bid.

This time, the long end is moving faster than the intervention.

Buybacks can absorb pressure from one direction.

A hawkish Fed + a renewed war premium is a different problem.

Watch what Treasury does next.

Does it escalate again... or let the 30-year find its own level?🍌

Two supertankers carrying Saudi crude were struck by projectiles while leaving the Strait of Hormuz.

At the same time, refinery disruptions across Russia and the Middle East are tightening refined-product markets, while Brent has pushed back above $91.

not risk — but barrels that may not be replaceable quickly.

#######POST 3 midday

ISM Manufacturing came in at 54.6% vs. 55.2% expected, with New Orders.... the leading component... falling three full points to 53.7%.

Yet the 10-year is still at 4.79%, its highest since January 2025.

A week ago, September hike odds were 39.6%.

Now they're 66.4%.

Normally, a weakening goods economy argues against tightening.

This time, it isn't winning.

The market is pricing oil + a rate path Warsh's silence already put in motion, not the manufacturing data in front of it.

The real test comes Thursday with ISM Services and Friday with jobs.

If neither can move 4.75%+, the question changes:

Has the 10-year stopped listening to data?


#######POST 4 midday

AI is competing with Treasury for the same long-duration capital.

$157B in investment-grade corporate bonds was sold in August alone, including Alphabet's $25B deal.

September's pipeline is projected at $175-250B.

Meanwhile, companies have borrowed more than $410B for AI buildout in the back half of this year.

All of that is hitting a market where the US savings rate is just 2.7%.

And you're seeing the pressure today:

$MRVL, $INTC and $SNDK are down more than 2.5%.

$NVDA, $AMZN and $TSLA are down over 1%.

The S&P is down 0.69%.

For three weeks, we've framed this as a fight between the Fed and Treasury.

It's now three forces pulling on the same long end:

- Monetary policy.
- Fiscal intervention.
- AI's appetite for capital.

Watch the AI complex as September's corporate issuance gets priced.

If AI-adjacent names keep underperforming, the market may not just be repricing rates.

It may be repricing what AI capex actually costs to fund.🍌
