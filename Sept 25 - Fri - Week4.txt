#######MARKET BRIEF SEPT 25, 2026

🌅 MARKET BRIEF — SEPT 25, 2026

This week didn't pick a driver. It proved one exists.

For five weeks, oil set the price of money. 

Wednesday broke that rule in a single session: the 10-year, 5-year and 30-year all crossed 2007-era levels at once, on a 62-month PMI high and Barr's hawkish line, not another barrel. 

Gold and Bitcoin, which had spent a month disagreeing about what risk even meant, sold off together... the clearest version of that convergence we've seen this cycle.

Oil spent the rest of the week arguing with itself instead. 

Houthi missiles hit Riyadh Thursday, Brent spiked past $108, then gave nearly the whole move back on an unconfirmed report of a phased Hormuz-for-blockade deal emerging from the UN talks. 

Iran has denied versions of this story before. 

The pattern this month has been consistent: 
→ anonymous sources move the price, 
→ named officials move it more, 
→ and on-record confirmation moves it most. 

Nothing on record yet.

The 10-year kept climbing through all of it. It touched 5.22% intraday Thursday and closed at 5.20%, its highest close since 2007. 

A weak 7-year auction, Japan's own 10-year hitting a 30-year high that morning, and oil's $4 round-trip still couldn't pull it back. 

This morning it's eased slightly to 5.17%... still well above where the week started.

That's the part to sit with this morning: the discount rate stopped waiting for oil's permission this week. 

→ A few basis points of overnight relief doesn't mean it needs oil's permission again.

Bitcoin is the one piece that hasn't confirmed the new regime. 

It's holding near $84,000, essentially where it sat before Wednesday's rate break, after briefly falling with gold. 

→ If it decouples from yields again by the close, Wednesday may have been the exception. 

→ If it keeps fading with gold, the convergence is becoming the new baseline.

Three questions remain before the weekend:

→ 5.17–5.20%.... does the pullback from Thursday's high extend, or was this morning's dip simply pre-weekend positioning?

→ Hormuz deal talk.... does Tehran or Washington confirm it, or does it quietly disappear like the other reports this month?

→ Gold and Bitcoin... do they separate again, or are they now reading the same discount rate?

Whichever breaks first will tell us which part of this week was signal.... and which part was simply the shock still working its way through the system. 🍌

#######POST 1 morning

Oil used to get the first word on rates. This week, the bond market stopped listening.

Brent spent Thursday swinging more than $8 between the Riyadh strike and the unconfirmed Hormuz deal report. 

The 10-year went the other way: 5.22% intraday, 5.20% at the close, and still 5.17% this morning.

That disconnect matters because the break wasn't caused by another barrel. 

Wednesday's 62-month PMI high and Barr's hawkish line pushed yields higher while oil was falling. 

Thursday then brought another full day of oil volatility... and rates still held the level.

That's four sessions with the old oil-to-duration relationship failing to reassert itself.

Now comes the cleaner test.

- No Treasury auction today. 
- No scheduled Fed voice. 

→  If 5.17% holds without either, the bond market may have stopped waiting for oil to tell it where rates belong. 🍌


#######POST 2 morning

Wednesday forced gold and Bitcoin into the same trade. 

Friday is already asking whether that was temporary.

The rate shock pushed both lower as the 5-year, 10-year and 30-year crossed 2007-era levels together. 

Thursday brought another fresh high in the 10-year, and both stayed under pressure.

This morning, the picture is different.

Bitcoin is holding near $84,000... roughly where it was before Wednesday's break... while gold remains soft. 

→  On a quiet Friday, the two aren't moving in lockstep.

That's the distinction to watch.

A shock can make two very different assets behave alike. 

→  The bigger question is whether they keep doing it after the shock stops getting stronger.

→  If $BTC  holds near $84,000 while gold stays weak, the old split is still alive. 

→  If both resume falling without a fresh rate shock, Wednesday's convergence was more than an exception. 🍌



#######POST 3 midday

5.17% didn't hold this morning.

The 10-year is back near 5.19%, closing in on Thursday's 5.20% close... and nothing on today's calendar is pushing it there. No auction. No Fed speaker. No fresh oil shock.

That's more important than the two basis points.

The level is climbing without needing a new catalyst to defend it. 

The market isn't waiting for oil to tell rates where to go.

The cleaner test now is 5.20%.

→  If the 10-year reaches it again into the close with the calendar still this quiet, Thursday's high wasn't just a reaction. It was a level the market is starting to accept. 🍌



#######POST 4 midday

The quiet test is turning into the more important one.

Bitcoin has slipped from roughly $84,000 to $83,000 since the open, moving in the same direction as gold... with no new yield print or fresh headline forcing either move.

That's different from Wednesday's version. 

The 5-year, 10-year and 30-year crossing 2007-era levels in the same session gave the market a maximum-size rate shock capable of pulling both assets together.

Today needed nothing.

That's the harder question for the old split. 

→  A relationship that appears only under a maximum-size shock is one thing. 

→  A relationship still holding two days later, on a quiet Friday, without a fresh catalyst, is something else.

Let's watch whether Bitcoin keeps falling with gold into the close. 

→  If it does, Wednesday wasn't just an exception... the convergence is starting to look durable. 🍌


#######POST 5 afternoon

The 10-year just printed 5.21%... and the equity tape is showing us who can absorb it.

The Nasdaq and the names carrying the AI trade are holding up reasonably well. 

The Russell 2000 isn't. 

Small caps are underperforming again as the 10-year makes a fresh high with no auction, Fed speaker or fresh oil shock behind the move.

That's the more precise read.

The growth trade hasn't broken. It has narrowed.

Mega-cap companies can still find buyers willing to look past a higher discount rate. 

Smaller businesses, where financing costs hit closer to the surface, aren't getting the same pass.

Let's watch that gap into the close.

→  If the Nasdaq keeps holding while small caps continue to weaken, the market isn't ignoring 5.21%. 

It's deciding who has to pay for it first. 🍌



#######POST 6 afternoon

$BTC  is holding the lift from $84,000 this afternoon, but today's flow data shows the first net outflow in six sessions... ending the stretch that had absorbed every prior dip, including Wednesday's rate shock.

That's a different signal from price.

A rising $BTC price alongside negative ETF flows doesn't show the same underlying demand that carried the market through the week. 

Short covering can lift price without bringing fresh capital into the market.

So the question has changed.

→  Can $BTC  hold $84K without the ETF buyer showing up?

Monday's flow print is the clean test. 

→  If price holds while flows stay negative, this week's ETF-demand story has lost its strongest confirmation. 🍌

The VIX fell 5.1% to 14.87 today, even as the 10-year touched 5.225% intraday before easing back toward Thursday's level.

→  That's an unusual combination.

A week that produced the VIX's biggest weekly jump in more than a year just ended with volatility being sold back down while the bond market was still carrying its biggest rate shock.

So the question isn't whether risk appeared this week. It did.

→  The question is whether the VIX was pricing a durable change in risk.... or simply the shock of the repricing itself.

→  If Monday opens near 14.87 without a fresh catalyst, this week's volatility spike looks more like an event premium. 

→  If the VIX immediately pushes back toward the week's highs, Friday's unwind didn't remove the underlying demand for protection. 🍌

#######MARKET CLOSE/CENSUS POST

MARKET STRUCTURE CENSUS — SEPT. 25, 2026

The week that broke the discount rate ended with the discount rate giving some of it back.

→  The 10-year touched 5.225% this morning, a fresh high since 2007. 

By the close, it was back near 5.16–5.18%, barely changed on the day.

Five sessions. Three fresh highs. And the yield finished almost exactly where the week's real break happened.

→  Oil is why the week ends differently than it traded through most of it.

Iran asked Washington to return to the June MOU... a named govt ask, not another wire-service leak... and Brent fell 2.1% to $104.32, while WTI fell 2.3% to $92.41.

All month, oil demanded physical evidence before giving diplomacy much credibility.

Today, it priced a request.

That's a real shift in what counts as credible. 

The question heading into next week isn't simply whether the Strait reopens. 

It's whether Washington answers on the record.

→  The VIX gave us the week's cleanest contradiction.

Bloomberg called this week's move its biggest weekly VIX jump in more than a year. 

Then Friday erased 5.1% of it, closing at 14.87... even as the 10-year was still printing fresh 2007-era highs before the afternoon fade.

A fear gauge that builds all week and unwinds in one session without a new catalyst wasn't simply pricing the yield.

It was pricing uncertainty about where the yield would stop.

→  Gold and Bitcoin spent the day refusing to settle their old split.

Bitcoin lifted while gold fell around midday. By the close, gold was up 0.54% near $4,321 while Bitcoin was down roughly 0.48% near $83,950.

We've treated this as the real-yields-versus-risk-appetite split throughout this cycle.

Today it flipped inside four hours.

That's not one relationship reasserting itself. It's two different trades occasionally lining up and occasionally separating, with no consistent lead.

→ Equities finished broader than they traded through most of the week.

The Dow led at +0.93%, ahead of the S&P's +0.51% and Nasdaq's +0.48%... the opposite of the narrow, mega-cap-led tape that had been absorbing the rate shock while small caps lagged.

So the week's real lesson isn't that yields broke 2007 levels, or that oil fell on a diplomatic request, or that the VIX spiked and unwound.

→ It's that each move proved reversible.

A rate level that took a 62-month PMI print and a hawkish Fed governor to establish gave some ground back on a quiet Friday.

Oil repriced a named govt request.

The VIX unwound without a new catalyst.

Gold and Bitcoin separated again once the extreme rate shock was gone.

Next week starts with the level still standing... but with much less obvious pressure keeping it there. 

Happy weekend! 🍌
