#######MARKET BRIEF SEPT 10, 2026

🌅 MARKET BRIEF — SEPT 10, 2026

Yesterday answered whether buyers would show up for the debt.

They did.

Today tests whether Treasury can make the yield listen.

Wednesday's $39B 10-year auction drew 79% indirect bidder participation, the highest bid-to-cover since 2016, while primary dealers were left holding just 4.3% of the offering.

Investors didn't hesitate to buy.

They just demanded 4.834% to do it — roughly 15bps above last month's auction.

Then Treasury tripled today's buyback operation to $6B.

The 10-year rose anyway, touching 4.85%, its highest since October 2023, before easing 1bp to 4.84% this morning.

That's the contradiction sitting in front of the market.

The intervention got bigger.

The yield didn't listen.

Today's $6B operation is therefore a harder test than yesterday's auction.

Does the buyback finally hold 4.84–4.85%?

Or has this level stopped responding to buyback size the way it did three weeks ago?

Oil is doing something it hasn't done in days:

Nothing.

Brent is holding near $101.25, essentially flat overnight, a session after closing above $100 for the first time since late July following the destruction of five Iranian tankers.

WTI sits near $96.13.

After a month in which every pause eventually broke toward another physical trigger, today's flatness is itself the question.

Is the market done pricing Wednesday's shock?

Or is it simply waiting for the next one?

Underneath both, the VIX remains inside the 15.7–16.5 range, still within a point of the 16.34 level it touched on September 2 — a session with no war headline attached.

Three losing equity sessions under two of the largest strikes of the war, and volatility still hasn't broken meaningfully above a quiet data day.

Gold near $4,431–$4,451 and Bitcoin near $78,300–$78,900 are both drifting soft this morning, extending Tuesday's split.

The safe-haven bid still hasn't shown up at scale on either side.

Then comes PPI at 8:30 AM.

August PPI is a backward-looking print arriving into a market that has spent the past week pricing September's war risk in real time.

ISM and ADP both failed to move this tape materially.

So the question isn't simply whether PPI beats or misses.

It's whether a hawkish PPI can do to the 10-year what a bigger buyback couldn't do yesterday. 🍌

This number measures conditions from before $101 oil, before the latest escalation, before the market spent this week repricing a live war in real time.

ADP missed by 9,000 jobs and the tape barely moved.

Two direct military strikes on U.S. warships didn't move the 10-year from 4.79%.

So PPI has a different job today.

It doesn't just need to surprise.

It needs to prove the market still cares about information that arrives after the event.

If PPI comes in hot and the 10-year still refuses to move, that's no longer just another muted data reaction.

It's a hierarchy.

The market is putting live physical risk above the economic calendar.

And that would tell us something much bigger than today's PPI number. 🍌
