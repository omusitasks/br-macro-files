#######POST 5 morning

Alibaba just raised $10.2 billion to fund AI spending. The placement was reportedly oversubscribed.

Its Hong Kong shares still fell more than 8%.

The money came in. The stock went the other way.

Last Wednesday, we watched something similar happen with SK Hynix. The company announced a massive treasury-share cancellation, Seoul sold it nearly 10%, while the US listing moved higher on the same disclosure.

Same news doesn't always meet the same market.

Sometimes the difference is sitting in the people already holding the position.

Alibaba's Hong Kong investors are now looking at fresh dilution after a stock that had already run hard. The buyers taking the placement are entering at a different price, with a different reason for owning it.

This is now the third time in five sessions we've seen the market remind us that the headline doesn't explain the price on its own. Positioning does.

Watch what happens when the US market opens. If the ADR refuses to follow Hong Kong lower, the gap itself may tell us more than today's 8% move. 🍌



#######POST 6 midday

Samsung just put up a number big enough to make almost any shareholder smile.

Up to 110 trillion won in capital returns.

The stock fell 9%.

The market wasn't arguing about how large the number was. It was looking at what wasn't inside it.

Investors wanted something concrete: a buyback, a cancellation, a commitment they could actually measure. Instead, Samsung gave them a range.

That uncertainty is now reaching beyond Samsung. Micron is down around 3%. SanDisk roughly 5%. SK Hynix around 3%.

Last week, SK Hynix showed us the other side of this trade. A real cancellation produced completely different reactions depending on who was holding the stock.

Today, the whole group is reacting to something much simpler:

A promise to return capital isn't the same thing as returning it.

Watch whether the group finds a floor before Wednesday. If it doesn't, Nvidia walks into an AI market that may already be looking for a reason to sell. 🍌


#######POST 7 midday

Friday, we left Bitcoin with one unanswered question.

Were people still buying—or were we just watching the aftershock of more than $1 billion in forced short liquidations?

The flows are starting to answer.

Bitcoin paused over the weekend, which briefly made Friday's move look like it might have run out of buyers. It didn't stay there. By Monday morning, it was climbing again toward $79,000.

Then the ETF numbers arrived.

$1.9 billion flowed into spot Bitcoin ETFs last week, the strongest week since October, following the $606 million single-day inflow we were already watching on Friday.

A squeeze can force people into buying once.

It can't keep sending new money through the door after the forced buyers have already gone.

That doesn't settle the entire Bitcoin story. ETF flows are still net negative for 2026.

But the specific evidence we said we needed on Friday is beginning to show up.

The buyers are still arriving after the squeeze ended.

Watch whether those flows survive Wednesday. PCE and Nvidia give this market its first serious test of the week. 🍌



#######POST 8 midday

The Treasury has now made this problem bigger three times.

First, it doubled long-bond buybacks to $4 billion.

Then officials suggested going even further.

Now, reports say the Treasury is considering using its roughly $950 billion General Account to buy bonds directly.

The 10-year is still sitting above where it was before any of this started.

That's the part becoming difficult to ignore.

Last week, one announcement was enough to knock yields down for a few hours. Then they came back.

Since then, the response has been more buybacks, bigger buybacks, and now potentially direct purchases.

Each step is larger than the last.

The market's answer hasn't been.

At some point, repeating the same reassurance stops being reassurance and starts raising a different question:

Why does the market need to be convinced this many times?

The next signal isn't another Treasury official talking about what they could do.

It's whether the General Account actually gets used—and what the 10-year does after real money, not another promise, enters the market. 🍌

It's why the market decided not to price it. 🍌
