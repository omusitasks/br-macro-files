#######MARKET CLOSE/CENSUS POST

MARKET STRUCTURE CENSUS — SEPT. 3, 2026

The market came into today with a hawkish problem.

ISM Services beat, while prices paid reached its highest 12-month average since April 2023. 

On paper, that is exactly the kind of inflation signal that should keep rate-cut hopes under pressure.

Instead, the market repriced the Fed's next decision.

Hike odds fell from 63.2% to 50.4% after Fed Governor Waller said he could support holding rates if inflation continues to cool... and pointed to next Thursday's CPI, not tomorrow's payrolls, as the number that will determine his vote.

The 10-year yield fell from 4.79% to 4.75%.

The Dow gained 635 points, its best session since Aug. 4.

The important move wasn't the size of the numbers. It was the change in what the market considered authoritative.

Three weeks of hawkish minutes, dissents and Jackson Hole language had built the case for higher-for-longer. 

One credible voice didn't erase that evidence. It changed which evidence traders were waiting for.

That is why tomorrow's payrolls report now enters a different market.

Not because labor suddenly stopped mattering... but because the rate market has already been given another reference point.

Oil offered the cleanest contrast.

Iran hit U.S. bases overnight. Brent barely moved because roughly 8M barrels a day are still transiting the Hormuz.

The geopolitical headline was real.

The physical supply constraint was what mattered.

Then earnings produced a third rule.

$AVGO beat revenue and earnings, but guidance came in $200M below consensus. The stock opened down as much as 5.26%.

$SNOW raised its own guidance and closed up more than 25%.

$AVGO eventually clawed back roughly half its loss, moving almost in lockstep with the falling 10-year yield.

$SNOW didn't need the yield move.

That's important.

The guidance mechanism we've been watching this month isn't simply:

Beat the quarter → stock falls if guidance disappoints.

There's another variable underneath it:

Cost of capital can rescue part of a guidance miss.

But it cannot manufacture a guidance beat that isn't there.

So today's market produced three different standards for evidence:

- Oil: physical flows.

- Rates: credible policy guidance.

- Earnings: forward expectations.

Tomorrow's payrolls report was supposed to be the week's decisive input.

It still matters.

But the market has already shown us that the number alone may not determine the reaction.

The question now isn't simply whether payrolls beats or misses.

It's whether the data can move the price on its own... or whether the market first needs someone credible to tell it how to read the data. 🍌
