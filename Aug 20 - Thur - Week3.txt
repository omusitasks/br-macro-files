#######(THURSDAY - AUGUST 20TH 2026)

#######POST 1 morning

Yesterday, we were waiting to see whether the Fed's hawkish minutes would eventually catch up with the market.

Overnight, something else took over.

Bitcoin jumped 11% above $71,000. Gold added 3%. More than $1 billion in Bitcoin shorts were liquidated within an hour, while the Treasury's expanded long-bond buyback programme continued pulling attention back toward liquidity.

Just two days ago, the 30-year yield touched 5.34%.

This morning, it's back below 5.2%.

That doesn't mean the Fed suddenly stopped mattering. It means the market received a more immediate force to trade: liquidity entering the long end while heavily positioned Bitcoin shorts were forced to cover.

The interesting question now is what remains after the forced buying disappears.

If Bitcoin can hold above $70,000 without the shorts doing the buying for it, this move becomes something more than a squeeze. 🍌


#######POST 2 morning

Trump said oil prices could “drop like a rock” if Iran gives in.

Overnight, Brent moved the other way.

It climbed toward $93, a fresh weekly high, after Trump announced what he described as the most severe economic operation yet against Iran and threatened consequences for countries helping Tehran bypass sanctions.

The contradiction isn't really a contradiction once you look at the timing.

A tougher sanctions regime may eventually force an outcome that brings more supply back to the market. But before that happens, the immediate question is whether Iranian barrels become harder to move.

Right now, the market is pricing that risk first.

Pressure can eventually bring prices down. Before it does, it can make supply tighter.

Watch China. If refiners there begin stepping back from Iranian crude, the market will have its first real evidence that the pressure is reaching physical supply rather than just generating headlines. 🍌



#######POST 3 morning

Two days ago, Target gave investors a strong quarter and still got sold.

This morning, Walmart is getting the same treatment.

Revenue beat. Full-year guidance went up. E-commerce grew 23%.

On the surface, that's exactly what a beat-and-raise quarter is supposed to look like.

Walmart is still down about 5% in premarket.

The part investors are struggling with sits further down the report. U.S. comparable sales grew 2.6%, below the 3.5% the market expected, while new drug-price caps took a 0.8% bite out of health and wellness. Then management guided next quarter's earnings to 62–64 cents, down from 74 cents this quarter.

We keep treating “beat and raise” as if it tells us one thing.

This week, the market has made it two questions: what did you actually do this quarter, and what happens after this one?

So far, investors seem far more interested in the second answer.

If management doesn't give investors more confidence during the call, this becomes the second retailer this week where the outlook mattered more than the beat. 🍌


#######POST 4 midday

Two hours ago, the story looked simple.

Treasury support pushed long-term yields lower, Bitcoin and gold rallied, and the market appeared to be trading one broad liquidity move.

That story is starting to split.

The 30-year yield is back above 5.2%. The 10-year is back near 4.69%. Gold is retreating as those yields recover.

Bitcoin isn't.

It's still sitting near $71,600, holding almost all of its overnight move while the rest of this morning's trade starts to reverse.

That leaves us with a different question than the one we woke up with.

Maybe Bitcoin was never just trading the Treasury's signal. Or maybe something underneath the move is now strong enough to absorb the reversal happening elsewhere.

If bonds keep moving back toward yesterday's levels and Bitcoin still holds above $70,000, today's divergence becomes the story. 🍌



#######POST 5 midday

This morning, oil was climbing on threats. Now the threats are starting to show up where the market actually cares: on the water.

Reports of China-linked tankers turning back near the Strait of Hormuz, a vessel reportedly struck near the route, and the UAE suspending financial and economic transactions with Iran have moved the story beyond another round of political statements.

Brent is now above $93, moving toward $94. WTI is above $88.

The important distinction is starting to change.

Markets can ignore a threat if nothing follows it. But once shipping routes, buyers, insurers, or actual barrels begin responding, the question becomes whether supply is being affected before any formal disruption is announced.

Watch the insurance market next. If war-risk premiums start rising materially for Hormuz transits, the oil move may have found something more durable than a headline. 🍌


#######POST 6 afternoon

The same message that calmed the bond market yesterday isn't working this afternoon.

On Wednesday, the Treasury announced it would expand buybacks of long-dated debt, and yields immediately pulled back from their highs.

This afternoon, Bessent raised the possibility of an even larger buyback and pointed toward a new fiscal plan.

Yields rose instead.

That changes today's story.

This morning, we could say Wednesday's relief was simply fading. That happens.

But this afternoon, the market heard another reason to expect support and still pushed yields higher.

Sometimes the second announcement isn't new information. It's the market showing you that the first one wasn't enough.

And Bitcoin is still holding above $72,000.

If its rally were only a reaction to Treasury support and falling yields, today's reversal should have hurt it too. So far, it hasn't.

Now the pressure shifts back to the Treasury: if words stop moving the bond market, does the next response have to be something bigger than words? 🍌


#######POST 7 afternoon

Bessent spoke about two markets this afternoon.

Only one seemed to believe him.

On oil, he questioned why crude had jumped so sharply after Trump's economic-pressure comments and suggested that "maximum pressure" did not necessarily mean a return to large-scale combat. Brent, which had touched $93 intraday, cooled through the afternoon. WTI gave back most of its earlier move.

Then he turned to bonds.

He said Treasury buybacks could exceed $4 billion and argued that current yields do not reflect the underlying fundamentals. The bond market kept selling anyway. The 10-year climbed toward 4.69%. The 30-year reached 5.24%.

Same official. Same interview. Two very different verdicts.

Oil traders heard a geopolitical explanation and adjusted their risk premium. Bond traders heard reassurance from the issuer of the debt they were already demanding more compensation to hold.

If oil keeps cooling while yields keep climbing, today's divergence may tell us something important: the market is willing to take the Treasury's word on geopolitics, but not yet on its own debt. 🍌



#######MARKET CLOSE/CENSUS POST

MARKET STRUCTURE CENSUS — AUG. 20, 2026

One official tried to calm two different markets today.

By the close, only one of them had listened.

Bessent said he wasn't sure why oil had jumped so sharply after Trump's economic-pressure comments toward Iran. Brent had pushed past $93 earlier in the day. By the close, it was back near $88.

Then he turned to bonds.

He said Treasury buybacks could exceed $4 billion and argued that yields no longer reflected the underlying fundamentals.

The 30-year kept climbing anyway.

Same official. Same afternoon. Completely different verdicts.

Oil traders heard something that could change tomorrow: the geopolitical risk may not become the conflict they feared. Bond traders heard the government promising that its own debt should cost less to hold — and, for now, they weren't convinced.

Bitcoin took a third path. It closed above $72,000, its best level of the week, holding its ground through both the oil reversal and the renewed rise in yields.

That's probably the more useful thing we learned today.

The same day's news can hit three markets, and each one can be trading a completely different mechanism.

Tomorrow, the question isn't simply where oil, yields, or Bitcoin go next.

It's whether those three separate stories begin moving back into one.🍌
