#######POST 2 morning

Friday's 12.63% jump in Reddit left us with one question: how much of that move actually belongs to the stock?

It closed at $178.09, holding the entire move through Friday rather than giving it back after the inclusion announcement.

But the biggest mechanical buyer hasn't arrived yet.

Index funds are expected to buy ahead of Tuesday's open, while more than 13% of the float is sold short, meaning some of Friday's move may have come from shorts covering rather than investors suddenly changing their view of the business.

And that distinction matters.

Piper Sandler actually cut its price target last week over weak US user growth.

So Monday gives us the price.

Tuesday gives us the cleaner test.

If Reddit can hold around $178 after the index buying is absorbed(i.e tommorow), the market is telling us something different.




#######POST 3 midday

Elon Musk's comment this morning sent three memory stocks 5–8% higher, even though the market had already been trading the same idea.

SanDisk jumped 8%, Western Digital 6%, and Micron 5% after Musk flagged a memory bottleneck.

Last Thursday, SanDisk's earnings pushed the thesis.

Last Friday, Anthropic's numbers reinforced it.

Now Musk repeats the story and the stocks jump again.

That's the interesting part.

When a familiar idea keeps producing fresh buying, the market may not be discovering anything new.

It may be getting increasingly positioned around the same expectation.

Now we watch what happens when there's nothing new left to repeat.

#######MARKET CLOSE/CENSUS POST

MARKET STRUCTURE CENSUS — AUG. 17, 2026

We spent the day waiting for the 10-year to break 4.75%.

It never did.

It closed almost exactly where it started, around 4.68%.

But the 30-year went somewhere the 10-year didn't: 5.29%, its highest level since 2007.

The 2-year fell. Oil broke above $90. Stocks turned lower. Yet chipmakers held their gains and Bitcoin climbed 2%.

That's not a market simply running from risk.

The last time we saw this kind of tension between the short and long end was 1994, when long-term yields moved ahead of the Fed's own signals and forced markets to listen to the bond market instead.

Today's move isn't 1994.

But the shape is familiar.

If the 10-year follows the 30-year toward a new high this week, we'll know today's split was more than a one-day move.
