#######POST 1 morning

Wall Street proved something twice this month. It has never proven the price is right.

→ Sept. 9's 10-year drew the strongest demand in almost a decade and still printed a higher yield. 

→ Sept. 15's 20-year drew softer demand and the 10-year held above 5% anyway. 

→ Now Wednesday's 5-year came in weak and closed the day at 5.00%.

That's three auctions, three different demand levels, one direction.

$44B of 7-year notes price at 1 PM into a curve already at 5.11% on the 10-year and 5.41% on the 30-year. 

The tail and the indirect bidder share are the numbers to watch, not the headline yield.

→ If demand weakens further and the curve barely moves, buyers have stopped being the variable that sets the price. 🍌


#######POST 2 morning

The rate shock just picked up a second address.

Japan reopened this morning after its holiday and immediately put its 10-year at a 30-year high. 

At the same time, the US 10-year sits near 5.11% and the 5-year holds at 5.00%.

Neither market needed the other's headline.

Two major bond markets reached multi-decade levels within hours of each other.

That's a harder coincidence to wave off than Germany's 3.5% print on Sept. 11.

→  The question now is whether Japan's move pulls global capital toward yen assets and away from Treasuries, 

→ or whether both markets are simply repricing the same thing: money is getting more expensive everywhere at once. 🍌


#######POST 3 morning

Oil spent this week teaching the market who gets to move the price.

- An anonymous leak moved Brent briefly. 
- Bessent moved it again. 
- Trump speaking on the record made the move stick. 

Then yesterday, even a 24-hour-old comment was enough to send Brent more than 6% lower in a single session.

This morning, there's no new comment at all.

The diplomacy that carried Monday and Tuesday has gone quiet. 

What's left is a Washington threat and a Tehran refusal to yield... both familiar shapes oil spent five weeks refusing to price on rhetoric alone.

Yet Brent is still holding near $102.

That's the test now. 

→  If it stays there without a fresh voice defending it, oil isn't grading sources anymore. It's carrying a floor on its own. 🍌



#######POST 4 afternoon

Oil just changed what it considers credible.

Pezeshkian told the UN that Tehran won't bow to threats and will restrict Hormuz navigation while the blockade stands. Head of state. On the record. Brent held above $106.

Then Reuters reported unnamed negotiators were exploring a phased deal: Tehran reopens the strait, Washington lifts the blockade.

No names. No govt confirmation.

Brent gave back the move to $105.69. 

WTI fell to $94.30, and the S&P erased its earlier drop.

→  The weaker source just outweighed the stronger one.

That's the interesting part. 

The market isn't necessarily ranking the speaker anymore. It's ranking the distance to an outcome.

→  A presidential threat describes the status quo continuing. 

→  An unconfirmed negotiation describes the status quo ending.

→  If Oman or Tehran confirms the phased-deal report, today's move was early positioning. If the report fades without confirmation, the market just showed how much resolution is worth before the deal even exists. 🍌


#######POST 5 afternoon

Yesterday needed a shock to make gold and Bitcoin move together.

Today they're doing it without one.

The 10-year is almost exactly where it closed yesterday, near 5.11%. 

October hike odds have drifted toward 70%, but there's been no new inflation or labor print driving that move. 

Oil has simply round-tripped about $4.

Yet gold is down 0.85% at roughly $4,250, while Bitcoin is down more than 2% around $84,400.

That's a different test from yesterday.

When the 5-year, 10-year and 30-year all broke 2007-era levels at once, the convergence could be dismissed as a one-session rate shock. 

Today the curve hasn't broken again, but the relationship is still holding.

We've been tracking the gold-Bitcoin split because gold had been responding more directly to real yields while Bitcoin often behaved more like a liquidity/risk asset.

Now the question is whether that distinction survives the next shock.

→  If oil becomes the next catalyst and $BTC  separates from gold, the old split is still alive. 

→  If another rate move hits and both sell together again, the market may be repricing both through the discount rate. 🍌


#######POST 6 evening

Treasury just ran the same $6B experiment again.

Same size. Same maturities. Different rate environment.

The 30-year pushed into 5.38–5.42% during the buyback window... the very stretch when the operation was supposed to provide support.

That makes today's result more useful than a one-off failed intervention.

The same $6B operation produced the same problem when tested earlier this month: 
→  Treasury hasn't changed the size of the tool while the market's required yield has moved higher.

And today the pressure came from both sides. Paulson, Hammack and Barkin all leaned hawkish, adding Fed pressure while Treasury was trying to lean against the curve.

The next operation matters more than today's headline.

→  If Treasury runs $6B again, the question is no longer whether the tool can move the market. It's whether the operation is deliberately too small for the rate environment it is trying to influence. 🍌


#######POST 7 evening

Bitcoin is falling. The buyers haven't left the building yet.

$BTC  is around $84K, extending today's 2.4% slide, with more than $280M in long liquidations forcing positions out.

But Wednesday's U.S. spot ETF flows showed another side of the tape: 
→  +$347M of net inflows, the fifth straight day, led by IBIT at $166M and FBTC at $143M.

Sept. 4 offered the same setup: price fell while ETF flows kept buying, but the gap closed within a day and flows proved the steadier signal.

Today's version is harder to dismiss because gold and Bitcoin are also falling together under rate pressure.

So there are now two forces to separate: leveraged sellers forcing price lower, and underlying buyers continuing to absorb supply.

→ Friday's ETF print is the clean test.

→ Another inflow day while BTC is falling would show the forced selling and underlying demand are coming from different populations. 

→ A turn to outflows would say the price decline is finally reaching the buyer base too. 🍌
