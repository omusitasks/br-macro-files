#######(TUESDAY - AUGUST 11TH 2026)

#######POST 1 morning

Oil closed above $83 for a fourth straight session, even after Iran said a deal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz was in its final stages.

You'd expect that headline to send oil lower. It didn't, because the market is no longer pricing just the possibility of a reopening. The U.S. Strategic Petroleum Reserve has fallen below 300 million barrels, its lowest level since 1983, while new demands from Washington make a quick resolution harder to price.

That's different from a headline risk that can disappear tomorrow. The supply cushion is actually smaller.

And with CPI tomorrow, oil is becoming part of the inflation story whether the market wants it there or not. 🍌



#######POST 2 morning

Bitcoin fell 1.4% Monday as oil jumped. That matters more than the 1.4%.

When the Hormuz standoff flared up again, Bitcoin sold off alongside growth stocks while gold pushed to a two-month high. If you still think of Bitcoin as trading like digital gold, yesterday was a useful reminder to watch the behavior, not the label.

Gold absorbed the uncertainty. Bitcoin traded like a risk asset.

That distinction tells you more about the marginal buyer than the day's headline does. The next oil spike will show whether Bitcoin can decouple from equities, or whether the same investors keep selling it when risk gets repriced. 🍌


#######POST 3 midday

Oil nearly touched $90 this morning before giving back more than $3. The Strait of Hormuz hadn't changed. A comment had.

A Pakistani official said the US and Iran were "close" to an agreement. There was no text, no signature, and neither Tehran nor Washington confirmed it. The market traded it anyway. Treasury yields jumped to their highest level this month, then gave the entire move back on the same unconfirmed comment.

That's the interesting part. We're watching billions move on statements that may never become agreements, while the underlying supply cushion hasn't moved: the Strategic Petroleum Reserve is still below 300 million barrels, its lowest level since 1983.

Tomorrow's CPI will show whether the market is pricing the next headline or the supply problem underneath it. 🍌



#######POST 4 midday

Coherent fell 14%. Lumentum fell 8%. The report that had helped send both stocks up 44% in five sessions hadn't changed by the time the selling started.

Neither company needed bad news to fall. Both were heading into earnings at valuations that left very little room for disappointment, while the broader AI-optics trade was selling off even in names with no direct exposure to the policy issue.

That's what makes the move interesting. When a stock is priced for a flawless quarter, the catalyst doesn't have to be bad. Sometimes investors simply stop paying the old price.

Lumentum reports tonight. Coherent reports tomorrow. Those results should tell us whether this was a valuation reset or the first crack in a much bigger trade. 🍌



#######POST 5 afternoon

Oil is flat. Yields have settled. Stocks have recovered most of Monday's dip. From the screen, this afternoon looks reassuring.

But nothing has actually been resolved. There is still no agreement on the Strait of Hormuz, and tomorrow's CPI hasn't happened.

What the market appears to be doing is pricing two favorable outcomes at once: softer inflation and de-escalation in the Middle East. Neither has arrived yet.

That's what makes today's calm worth watching. If CPI comes in hot while the Iran talks remain stuck, investors won't have one assumption to unwind. They'll have two.

The important number tomorrow may not be the CPI itself. It may be how much of today's calm survives after 8:30 a.m. ET. 🍌



#######POST 6 afternoon

The S&P 500 barely moved today. Roughly 300 of its companies didn't get that memo.

We tend to read a flat index as a flat market. It isn't. Underneath the unchanged headline, Meta climbed while Alphabet fell, even as both remain exposed to the same broader AI spending story.

That's the problem with reading the index alone. An index is an average of hundreds of different positioning decisions, expectations and capital flows.

The S&P can tell you the market barely moved. It can't tell you where the money actually went.

Before calling today quiet, look underneath the average. 🍌




#######MARKET CLOSE/CENSUS POST

Nothing got confirmed today.

Oil didn't get a signed deal. Tomorrow's CPI hasn't printed. Lumentum hasn't reported. Yet the market spent the day trading all three as if the answers were already known.

A Pakistani official said a deal was close, and yields spiked before giving the move back. Stocks recovered as expectations for a soft CPI grew. Lumentum's stock was repriced before investors had even seen the results.

Three different markets. One mechanism: expectations moved before reality arrived.

That's the part worth carrying into tomorrow. Calm doesn't necessarily mean the market has found certainty. Sometimes it means everyone is positioned around the same answer and waiting for the number to prove them right.

CPI at 8:30 a.m. ET is the first real test. Watch what breaks when expectation meets reality. 🍌
