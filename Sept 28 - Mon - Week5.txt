#######POST 5 afternoon

Let's have a close read of what rates just told us this afternoon.

There was no Treasury auction, scheduled Fed voice nor economic print to complicate the picture today... just oil moving, and rates moving with it.

That's important because last week we thought the old oil-to-duration relationship had finally broken.

Well, today's tape put that conclusion right back on trial.

The 10-year finished at 5.21%, its highest close since mid-2007, while the 30-year pushed to 5.57%, a level last seen in 2004.

So, for now, the cleanest single-variable test we've had this month points the other way: oil still has a seat at the table when the price of money moves.

But Tuesday changes the setup.

Musalem, Goolsbee and Williams are back on the calendar, and if 10Y stays around 5.21% with Fed voices back in the room, we'll know today's move wasn't simply oil borrowing the mechanism for a day.

→  It will mean oil never really lost it. 🍌

Well, here we go again.

Take away the auction, the Fed and the data, leave oil... and duration still moves.

→  The 10-year is now sitting within 6 basis points of the actual June 12, 2007 print of 5.303%.

→  Gold then gave us another familiar split, only harder this time.

October hike odds moved above 70%, gold fell 3.54% to $4,168.40, a seven-week low, while $BTC fell roughly 1.2%.

Remember Sept. 16? We asked whether Bitcoin would keep ignoring the rate shock that was repricing gold.

Today's shock was larger.

→  Bitcoin still absorbed only about a third of gold's decline.

So the real-yields-versus-liquidity split we've been tracking throughout the cycle didn't disappear when the rate shock got bigger. If anything, the gap became more visible.

→  And then the equity tape pulled another little trick.

The S&P fell 0.77%, Nasdaq 0.92% and the Dow just 0.67%. On the surface, that's a fairly ordinary down day.

That's the part the index doesn't explain.

→  an index can tell us the market barely moved without telling us where the money actually went.

Today made that distinction pretty hard to miss.

So, underneath one quiet-looking session, four different things happened:

- gold reacted much harder than Bitcoin to the rate shock, 
- Bitcoin maintained the liquidity side of its split, and
-  the headline indexes understated how weak breadth actually was.

Tomorrow is where these threads get tested.

Musalem, Goolsbee and Williams put the Fed back into the rates equation. Boeing opens with the FAA timeline still unresolved. And 5.303% is now less than 6 basis points away.
