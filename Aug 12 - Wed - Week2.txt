#######(WEDNESDAY - AUGUST 12TH 2026)

#######POST 1 morning

Friday's jobs report said the labor market lost 23,000 jobs. This morning, another survey is telling us small businesses are planning to hire at the fastest pace since October 2022.

The NFIB optimism index climbed to 99.8, its highest reading in a year, driven largely by hiring intentions. That's a very different signal from the payrolls report, which markets used to reinforce the weakening-labor-market story.

Neither survey tells the whole story. But when they diverge this sharply, today's question is which one the market has already priced.

Watch whether upcoming jobs data starts moving toward what small businesses are saying now. 🍌



#######POST 2 morning

Several Fed officials have kept the possibility of a September hike alive. Stock futures are pointing higher anyway, with CPI only 30 minutes away.

That's not necessarily a contradiction. It is a bet.

The market appears to be giving more weight to today's inflation number than to what Fed officials have been saying. Beth Hammack said this week that bringing inflation down may require more than one hike, and she's no longer alone in that view.

Friday's weak jobs report pushed markets toward rate-cut expectations. It didn't settle the Fed's next move.

CPI does.

In 30 minutes, we'll find out whether investors were right to trust the data more than the officials. 🍌



#######POST 3 midday

CPI came in exactly as expected this morning. Gold went up anyway.

Stocks rose too, with S&P 500 and Nasdaq futures climbing on the same number. That looks strange until you separate what each market was actually pricing.

Stocks got relief from a benign inflation print and a strong run of AI earnings. Gold was responding to the overnight Navy engagement in the Gulf of Oman, a completely different risk.

Two assets moved together on the same screen, but they weren't trading the same story.

That's worth remembering the next time gold and stocks rise together. Correlation on the chart doesn't always mean agreement underneath it. 🍌


#######POST 4 midday

The US Navy confirmed it disabled a cargo ship for trying to break the blockade on Iranian ports. Oil fell.

That's the part worth paying attention to. A confirmed military action should add to the risk premium, yet Brent, which touched near $90 overnight, has given almost all of that move back. WTI is now lower on the day, while US crude inventories rose 9.1 million barrels this week, the largest build since February.

We've spent five sessions watching escalation push oil higher. Now the escalation arrived and the price went the other way.

That suggests the market may have already priced much of this risk. The next question isn't whether the conflict escalates again. It's whether new information can still push oil beyond what investors have already positioned for. 🍌


#######POST 5 afternoon

CPI came in exactly as forecast this morning. Rate traders barely reacted.

That's the interesting part. September hike odds slipped to about 44%, from 48% before the number and 54% a week ago, but the move was modest even after a softer-than-June inflation print. Gold is still holding above $4,400, while the rate market is barely changing its view.

The number arrived. The big repricing didn't.

We've spent the week watching every inflation and oil headline for the catalyst that would settle the Fed debate. This morning gave us a cleaner test: when CPI finally arrived, expectations barely moved.

Watch whether hike odds continue drifting lower into tomorrow's PPI or stall around the same range. If they stall, the market may be telling us that one soft inflation print isn't enough to change the Fed story. 🍌



#######POST 6 afternoon

Oil shrugged off yesterday's Navy strike. It isn't shrugging off six deaths.

Iran-backed Houthi rebels killed six people on a vessel in the Red Sea today, the first fatalities from these attacks in more than a year. WTI is back around $83.70 and Brent near $89.24, reversing the brief "oil eases" move from this morning.

We've been treating this conflict as one risk premium that simply grows or fades with each headline. Today's price action suggests the market may be distinguishing between different kinds of escalation: military action is one thing; confirmed fatalities are another.

But that's still a hypothesis. The real test is whether oil holds this afternoon's gain into the close. If it does, are we watching a new risk premium being priced — or just another geopolitical spike waiting to fade? 🍌



#######MARKET CLOSE/CENSUS POST

BR MARKET CLOSE — Wednesday, August 12, 2026

CPI landed exactly on target today: 3.4% headline, 2.5% core, both matching forecasts. That was supposed to be the day's big decision point. It barely moved anything. September hike odds slipped only slightly, from around 48% before the print to roughly 44%.

Then a Red Sea attack killed six people, the most serious escalation we've seen this week. Oil spiked to $83.70 before giving the entire move back, closing around $82.60, down 0.7%. The VIX fell 4.8% to 14.55 instead of rising. Bitcoin finished roughly flat near $63,550, despite a nervous options market underneath it. Stocks still closed higher, with the S&P 500 up about 0.3%.

Three things arrived today that looked capable of changing the market's story: a clean inflation print, a fatal geopolitical escalation, and nervous derivatives positioning. None materially changed it.

That's the finding.

The market came into today already positioned around a story, and today's information wasn't enough to force a meaningful repricing. CPI didn't settle the Fed debate. The Red Sea attack didn't create a lasting oil premium. Even Bitcoin's options market showed more tension than the spot price itself.

We've spent the week waiting for the next event to resolve the position. So far, it hasn't. Not Tuesday's unconfirmed headline. Not today's CPI. Not today's escalation.

Thursday's PPI is next. Will it finally change what the market believes, or will it become another piece of information absorbed without changing the trade? 🍌
